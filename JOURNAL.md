## 2026-11-25: Pivot from reading leases to recovering deposits #pivot #decision

Reviving the project meant facing the weak spot in the original idea. Telling a tenant that a clause is void before they sign changes little: in a tight market the lease is take-it-or-leave-it, and few tenants can afford a lawyer to fight it later. Two facts point somewhere better. A void clause never needs a lawsuit from the tenant, because the landlord is the one who has to use it, so the useful moment is when the landlord asks for money. And security deposit disputes are built for people without lawyers: the law sets a deadline (21 days in Connecticut under § 47a-21(d), 30 days plus a sworn itemized list in Massachusetts under c. 186 § 15B(4)), cases go to small claims court, and a letter that cites the statute often settles things. The product now centers on getting the deposit back: a move-in record with timestamped photos, a deadline tracker at move-out, a cited demand letter, and a small claims packet as the last step. The lease scan stays as the background index that matches a dispute to its clause. Move-out is also when tenants have the least to fear from pushing back. Narrowing to deposit rules also shrinks the legal review ask to about ten rules a state, which may get past the intake triage that stalled every earlier review request. Demand letter templates must pass legal-aid review before they ship.

## 2026-06-16 — Market-vision angle committed: "Lease-as-a-Bet" #decision

Drafted the lofty market vision against Counterpart's "3-Hit Vision" framework (impossible goal / anti-theory / 25-year pitch) and committed to the **Lease-as-a-Bet** angle: a standard lease isn't a settled contract, it's a *wager*the drafter often places on the tenant's ignorance — and a large share of the boilerplate is intimidation, not enforceable law, priced on the bet you won't read it or fight it. The angle is load-bearing on a fact already in the repo: Conn. Gen. Stat. § 47a-4(b)*flatly* declares the (a)(1)–(10) clauses "unenforceable," and those exact clauses (waive-all-rights, confession of judgment, hold-harmless, self-help eviction, distraint) keep getting printed into leases anyway — the law says void, the lease says signed. The smoking-gun corroboration is the team's own market-research line: landlords "may include illegal clauses... knowing that students are transient and unlikely to pursue legal action." Impossible goal landed on **"Make the fine print bet against itself."** Anti-theory (the uncomfortable, conference-unsayable truth): *the unenforceable clause is a feature, not a drafting error — the rental industry's standard contract is partly priced on the statistical certainty that most tenants never read, never challenge, and never collect.*Held everything inside the hard constraints — the vision describes a market transformation (private contract → publicly legible contract) and never claims LeaseLookout gives legal advice or decides what the law is; it only makes the gap between "printed" and "enforceable" visible and cited. Refined ICP starts at the Wesleyan/Middletown first-lease signer. Flagged the live risk for Yinka to weigh: this framing is sharper-edged than the neutral "defensive API" line and will read as adversarial to landlords/PropTech — fine for a stance, but keep the*product copy* citation-first and non-accusatory so it survives a UPL sniff test and doesn't name specific parties as bad actors.
